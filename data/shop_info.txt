CHOOSING A CRICKET BAT
Ask about the player's age or height, playing level, preferred shot style, and whether the bat will be used with leather or tennis balls. Bat size, pickup, balance, weight, handle shape, and willow type affect suitability. A heavier bat is not automatically better; the player should be able to control it comfortably.
English willow and Kashmir willow are different materials and can vary in price, feel, grain appearance, and performance. Do not promise that one bat will improve performance. Customers should check manufacturer specifications and, where possible, handle the bat before choosing.
Bat size should be selected according to the player's height, age, and comfort rather than age alone. If unsure, ask for height and age and recommend checking the manufacturer's sizing chart. Do not invent exact sizing measurements when the relevant chart is unavailable.

PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT
Correctly fitting helmets, pads, gloves, and other protective equipment can help reduce injury risk. Choose the correct size, follow manufacturer fitting instructions, and replace damaged equipment. Do not modify protective equipment in a way that compromises its function. For equipment that has suffered a significant impact or has visible damage, follow the manufacturer's inspection and replacement guidance.

CRICKET BALLS AND FOOTWEAR
The appropriate ball depends on format, age group, conditions, and competition rules. Leather balls are commonly used in formal cricket, while softer practice balls may suit training or tennis-ball cricket. Customers should confirm the required ball type with their club, coach, or competition.
Cricket footwear should suit the playing surface and role. Spiked footwear may suit some grass pitches, while other soles may be better for artificial or indoor surfaces. Check venue rules and manufacturer guidance.
